Weekly programming calendar — 6 courts

6:00am–10:00pm, every day, across all 6 courts. When two or three activities share the same time slot, they sit side by side rather than stacked, so every block's top and bottom edge lines up exactly with its real start and end time on the hour axis — nothing is left looking shorter than it actually runs. The exact court split is still stated in the label (e.g. "2/6 courts"). Unprogrammed courts in that same slot are simply not labeled — they stay available for private lessons, court rental, or casual play. Long blank stretches (early morning, mid-afternoon) are fully open across all 6 courts on purpose.

Social play is level-specific Mon–Thu, using courts the leagues aren't on: Mon & Wed evenings run Level A and Level B as two separate groups; Tue & Thu evenings (on the courts Women's League and Box League leave free) run Level C. Friday, Saturday, and Sunday socials open all three level groups at once instead of one all-levels block — that's deliberate, to measure real demand per level before deciding whether to make those level-specific too. Every level group gets the same amount of social time each night — the split happens by court, never by shortening one group's hours. The format itself (Americano, Mexicano, King of the Court, etc.) rotates — see the Social Play style menu in the Program Playbook below. Friday nights also rotate a themed dynamic on top of that format rotation — Ladies Night, Industry Night, Couples/Date Night, Trivia Night, and more, drawn from what already works at other padel clubs, Topgolf, bars, and hotels (see the Program Playbook below). Click a legend chip to isolate a program type.

Intro to Padel is now on-demand, not a fixed slot — booked like a private lesson whenever a newcomer wants it. Saturday and Sunday mornings also push King of the Court and Ladder Challenge Play earlier and split them by court instead of running one at a time (e.g. Ladder Challenge on 2 courts while a Trio Pod cohort uses 1, at the same time). Each weekend day adds one vendor-partnered Activation slot — breakfast & padel, recovery/wellness, and similar — built as a 30-minute session followed by 60–90 minutes of play.

Developmental Clinics — two days a week per level, at a real 4:1 ratio: Level A meets Monday morning and Wednesday evening, Level B meets Wednesday morning and Friday evening, Level C meets Thursday morning and Saturday late-morning. Every clinic runs at a 4:1 coach-to-player ratio now, not 6:1 — that tighter ratio is reserved for these developmental sessions only. A 4-week technical rotation (footwork & positioning → wall & glass play → bandeja/vibora/smash → doubles positioning & shot selection) repeats on both of a level's days, but the second day is offset two weeks into the cycle, so it never teaches the same theme as the first day in the same week. A player who only ever comes on one of the two days still completes the full 4-theme curriculum in 4 weeks; a player who comes both days completes it in 2 and gets extra reps on each theme over the full month. See the Seasonal curriculum guide below for the exact rotation table. Purpose Clinic is now three fixed, level-open sessions — Cardio Padel (Friday), Strength Padel (Saturday), HIIT Padel (Sunday) — and keeps the more flexible 6:1 ratio since it's fitness-framed rather than technical.

Foundations, Progression, and Performance now each run two generations a week, both on weekdays — Level A: Monday + Wednesday 5:30–7pm; Level B: Tuesday + Friday 5:30–7pm; Level C: Thursday + Tuesday 5:30–7pm. Both generations of a level teach the identical week's curriculum (this is a sequential season, not a repeating rotation, so there's nothing to offset) — a player picks whichever day suits their schedule, or pays the discounted "double-pace" add-on to attend both and finish the season in half the calendar weeks. This doubles sellable seats per level per season without requiring anyone to add a new day to their week.

Three leagues — Bronze (Level A), Silver (Level B), Gold (Level C) — each run two nights a week instead of one: Bronze Fri 8:30–10pm + Sat 7:30–9pm; Silver Mon 8:30–10pm + Thu 8:30–10pm; Gold Tue 8:30–10pm + Sun 7–8:30pm. Every league night is deliberately scheduled opposite that level's two Developmental Clinic days, never on top of them. These league nights also replace the generic "Extended Social Play" filler blocks that previously sat in those same evening windows — the same court time now sells as structured league play instead of unstructured drop-in social.

Levels & seasonal development programs ("Generations")

Every player sits in one of three level bands. Regular weekly clinics are always available at each level; the three seasonal cohorts below are a 6–8 week committed group — same coach, same group, every week — designed to build a real "generation" of players who graduate together. Each cohort now runs as two separate generations per week — Generation 1 and Generation 2 meet on different days, same curriculum, same price — so a player picks whichever day fits their schedule, and the club can enroll two full cohorts per level instead of one. Players who want extra reps can join both generations for a discounted "double-pace" add-on, finishing the same season in half the calendar weeks with double the court time. The Trio Pod is a fourth, smaller Generations format: a self-formed group of 3 committing to each other and one coach for 4 sessions.

Developmental Clinics moved from a ~4–6:1 ratio to a real 4:1 ratio — fewer players share the coach's hourly cost, so those prices step up accordingly; Purpose Clinics keep the looser 6:1 ratio and stay at the old price point.

Participant & enrollment targets

Two different kinds of numbers below. Session capacity is a hard ceiling set by courts × ratio (or standard round-robin sizing) — you physically cannot sell more than this for a single session. Target enrolled / season is the realistic sales goal per program: usually 75–100% of the combined capacity across that program's weekly sessions, since a 100%-sold room every single week isn't a realistic ongoing target. Where a program now runs twice a week (Developmental Clinics, Foundations/Progression/Performance, Bronze/Silver/Gold), the target is for the two sessions combined, not each one separately.

Program playbook — how to run & market each activity

A resumé for every activity on the calendar: format and ratio, how it should be run day-to-day, and the marketing angle to use for it. Grouped by category.

Clinics & skill-building

Intro to Padel — now on-demand

60–75 min · 1 court · max 4:1 · no level required · booked on-demand, not a fixed calendar slot

How it's run: Booked exactly like a private lesson — whenever a newcomer wants it, not on a published weekly time. One coach walks a small group through grip, scoring, and live rallying so everyone hits real balls in the first 15 minutes; the coach personally books the player's next Open Play or Beginner Clinic before they leave. Moving it off the fixed calendar frees Saturday and Sunday's first morning slot for the new Purpose Clinics below.

Marketing: Target people who've never touched a paddle — friends of members, corporate wellness partners, "first-timer" social content. Priced as a loss-leader ($25 flat); promote as "book your first session any time" rather than pointing to a specific weekly slot.

Developmental Clinics (Level A/B/C) — 2 days/week, 4:1

90 min · 2 of 6 courts · 4:1 ratio · Level A: Mon AM + Wed PM · Level B: Wed AM + Fri PM · Level C: Thu AM + Sat late-AM

How it's run: This replaces the old scattered Daytime/Weekly/Weekend clinics with one clean system: every level gets exactly two fixed clinic days a week, always at a real 4:1 coach-to-player ratio (tighter than the old 6:1 — see Pricing). Both days teach the same 4-week technical rotation, but the second day is offset two weeks into the cycle, so a player never sees the same theme twice in the same week even if they come both days. Come once a week for 4 weeks and you get the complete 4-theme curriculum; come twice a week and you finish it in 2 weeks with extra reps by week 4. See the exact rotation table in the Seasonal curriculum guide below.

Marketing: Sell two enrollment options off the same product: a 4-session pass (pick one day, either week's start point works) or an 8-session pass (both days, "the full program in half the time"). Message: "know exactly what you're working on, and exactly how to get the full arc even on your busiest weeks."

Corporate Lunch Clinic

60 min · 2 of 6 courts · Level A/B or B/C · 4:1 ratio, fast-paced

How it's run: Scheduled inside a standard lunch hour for nearby office workers; higher tempo and less standing-around than a leisure clinic so it fits the time box.

Marketing: Target nearby office parks/corporate partners directly. It's also the natural feeder into the Corporate League — pitch both together to HR/wellness contacts.

Trio Pod (3+1)

1.5 hr · 1 of 6 courts · 3 committed players + 1 coach · 4-session package

How it's run: A pre-formed group of 3 books the same coach and slot for 4 weeks running. Session 1 is a shared assessment; sessions 2–3 target the group's specific gaps; session 4 is match-play integration plus a personalized takeaway plan.

Marketing: Target members who already play together informally. Pitch as "your own private squad" — a more personal, premium step up from a group clinic. Promote directly to friend-groups spotted playing Open Play together.

Purpose Clinics — 3 fixed fitness formats, 6:1 ratio

Down from four rotating technical themes to three fixed, level-open fitness formats — Cardio Padel, Strength Padel, and HIIT Padel — each with its own permanent weekly slot instead of rotating through a shared one. Freeing up that fourth purpose-clinic slot is what made room for the Developmental Clinics' second weekly day above. Purpose Clinics are the one place a looser 6:1 ratio still applies, since the format is fitness-framed rather than technical correction.

Cardio Padel

90 min · 2 of 6 courts · all levels · 6:1 ratio · Friday 3:00–4:30pm

How it's run: Coach-led, high-repetition drills prioritizing movement and heart rate over technical correction — constant motion, minimal standing around, same coaching skillset as a regular clinic with a different pace.

Marketing: Target the fitness-first buyer who won't sign up for a "padel class" but will sign up for a "workout." Cross-promote through gym/wellness partnerships using calorie/heart-rate messaging, not skill messaging.

Strength Padel

90 min · 2 of 6 courts · all levels · 6:1 ratio · Saturday 8:00–9:30am

How it's run: Bodyweight and resistance-band strength intervals worked between padel points — lunges, lateral bounds, med-ball rotational throws — built around the exact movement patterns padel demands (split-step, lateral push-off, rotational power), not a generic gym circuit.

Marketing: Position as "the only strength class that also makes you better at padel." Good cross-sell with the adjacent Ladder Challenge Play and Trio Pod happening the same morning — all three share the "getting the most out of the hour" framing.

HIIT Padel

90 min · 2 of 6 courts · all levels · 6:1 ratio · Sunday 8:00–9:30am

How it's run: Short, high-intensity padel-movement intervals (sprint-to-net, shadow-rally footwork, reactive ball-feed drills) with brief rest windows — the highest heart-rate-per-minute format of the three, aimed at the "I want to sweat, not just rally" crowd.

Marketing: Pair with the 2nd weekly Ladder Challenge Play session right after: "get your heart rate up at 8, then prove it on the ladder by 9:30." Cross-promote through HIIT/bootcamp studio partnerships (see Activations & Vendor Partnerships).

Seasonal Generations (see full curriculum below)

Foundations / Progression / Performance cohorts — 2 generations/week

6–8 wk season · 2 of 6 courts per generation · same coach + same group every week, by level band · Level A: Mon + Wed · Level B: Tue + Fri · Level C: Thu + Tue — all weekday evenings

How it's run: Cohort-based, sequential curriculum ending in a graduation match that places players directly into Open Play, the Ladder, or a Box League flight at their level. Each level now runs two parallel generations a week instead of one — Generation 1 and Generation 2 are two separate friend-groups on two different days, both following the identical week-by-week curriculum (unlike the Developmental Clinic's offset rotation, a cohort's two generations are in sync, not staggered, since the content is sequential rather than repeating). This doubles sellable seats per level per season without adding new visit days for anyone — a prospect just picks whichever day suits them. A player who wants extra reps can pay the discounted "double-pace" add-on and attend both generations the same week, finishing the season's curriculum in half the calendar time with double the coached hours.

Marketing: Target Developmental Clinic graduates directly, especially the 8-session (both-days) players — they're already primed for the "double-pace" pitch. This is the upsell from "drop-in" to "committed" — sold on a real peer group ("your generation") and a guaranteed next step after the season ends.

Social & open play

Social Play — style menu

90 min · rotating format, not a fixed one — see schedule below

How it's run: "Social" is the umbrella — the actual format rotates so regulars don't get the same night twice in a row. Toolkit for coordinators to rotate through:

Americano — partners rotate randomly each round, individual scoring. Easiest to explain to newcomers, good default.

Mexicano — partners re-paired each round based on current standings (winners with winners). Keeps matches close as the night goes on; best for a slightly more competitive crowd.

King of the Court — winners move up a court, losers move down. High energy, easy to spectate — save for the busiest/highest-energy night.

Round Robin — every pair plays every other pair once. Calmest, most predictable format — good for smaller or newer groups.

Team Scramble — random doubles re-draw each round, purely social framing (no standings). Best for Friday's date-night crowd.

Marketing: Advertise the week's format in advance (e.g. "Tuesday is Mexicano night") so regulars know what they're signing up for, and rotate on a set cycle (Americano → Mexicano → King of the Court → repeat) so the same level-day never goes stale.

Level-specific Social Days

Mon–Thu evenings, 7:00–8:30pm · uses courts the leagues aren't on

How it's run: Monday and Wednesday run Level A (1.0–2.2) and Level B (2.2–3.2) as two separate groups on the same evening — Monday has the full court allocation to itself, Wednesday shares the evening with Women's League and uses only the courts League isn't on. Tuesday and Thursday run Level C (3.2+) the same way, sharing Thursday with Box League. Friday, Saturday, and Sunday deliberately stay all-three-groups-at-once instead of one all-levels block or one level-specific block — that's the demand-sensing period.

Marketing: Once Fri/Sat/Sun attendance data shows which level shows up most on which day, convert the highest-demand pairing into its own dedicated day, the same way Mon–Thu already work. Track this from week one — see Staffing & Implementation below.

Round Robin Social Mixer

90 min · 2 of 6 courts · every pair plays every other once

How it's run: Coordinator-run, calmer pace than the evening socials — a good landing spot for beginner clinic graduates who want match play without evening-mixer energy.

Marketing: Target newer players and a slightly older/less-competitive crowd than the Friday night crowd. Position Sunday morning as the "easy, unhurried" match-play option.

Friday Night — Themed Rotation

Layered on top of the 7:00–8:30pm & 8:30–10:00pm Friday socials · one theme per week, 8-week cycle

How it's run: Every Friday keeps the usual Social format rotation (Americano, Mexicano, etc.) but also carries a weekly theme that changes the vibe without changing the court logistics. The lineup below is pulled from what already reliably works in hospitality and other racquet clubs — not invented from scratch — with the source noted for each:

Ladies Night — the single most common recurring promotion at bars and venues like Topgolf (discounted entry/a welcome drink for women), but run the way padel clubs are actually doing it now: built around skill-building and community, not just a price gimmick.

Industry Night — borrowed directly from the restaurant/bar world's standard service-industry discount night. Doubles as the easiest upsell path for our own Industry League members (servers/bartenders free before their shift) to bring a co-worker on their night off.

Couples / Date Night — padel clubs are already marketing "padel date nights" as an active, social, surprisingly-romantic alternative to dinner-and-a-movie; pairs register together, scoring stays casual, lounge seating after.

Trivia Night, Padel Edition — bar trivia is the most reliable weekly-regulars draw in hospitality (cheap to run, builds a loyal returning group); we run a short team trivia round in the lounge between padel rounds, prize for the winning team.

Retro / Decade Night — combines two proven ideas: padel clubs' "retro tournaments" (dress in classic gear, best-costume prize) and bars' decade-themed nights (a set playlist per decade).

Guest Pro Exhibition — a touring or local pro plays exhibition points and runs a 15-minute mini-clinic before open play starts; a differentiator racquet clubs have that bars and Topgolf-style venues don't.

Vinyl / DJ Social — modeled on hotel rooftop-bar programming (a live DJ, a more lounge-than-competitive vibe); leans social, good for a slower week or a rain-check crowd.

Charity / Community Night — padel clubs' "charity clinic" model: a portion of the night's court fees (or a raffle) benefits a local cause or partner, run with a nonprofit or neighborhood group.

Marketing: Announce next week's theme at the end of the current Friday night and again mid-week, so it functions like a standing appointment (e.g. "first Friday of the month is Guest Pro night"). Rotate the eight themes on a set 8-week cycle rather than choosing ad hoc, so planning, vendor bookings, and any guest-pro appearances can happen a month or two out.

Weekend mornings & vendor activations

Weekend Morning Restructure — King of the Court & Ladder Challenge

Sat 8:00–11:00am, Sun 8:00–11:00am · split by court, not run one-at-a-time

How it's run: Both formats now start earlier and share the floor with everything else that morning instead of taking it over sequentially. Saturday 8:00–9:30am splits 6 courts three ways — Purpose Clinic: Strength Padel (2), Ladder Challenge Play (2), Trio Pod (1) — then 9:30–11:00am splits again: Developmental Clinic Level C Day 2 (2) and King of the Court (4). Sunday mirrors the idea at smaller scale: a 2nd weekly Ladder Challenge session and a 2nd, smaller King of the Court sit alongside Purpose Clinic: HIIT Padel, Round Robin Mixer, and Masters. The principle: never give one format the whole floor when it can share courts with something else and reach more players in the same hours.

Marketing: Promote the earlier start as "beat the heat / beat the crowd" — first-thing weekend energy — and cross-sell: someone dropping a kid at Purpose Clinic can jump on Ladder Challenge on the adjacent court.

Vendor Activations — Breakfast & Padel / Recovery & Wellness

Sat 12:00–1:30pm & Sun 12:30–2:00pm · 2 of 6 courts · 30-min session + 60–90 min play

How it's run: A rotating outside partner (local coffee/breakfast spot, a recovery or nutrition brand, a gear maker) runs a short 30-minute activity — a tasting, a mini-clinic, a recovery demo — immediately followed by 60–90 minutes of hosted social play so the partner gets real face time with players, not just a table in the lobby. Saturday leans breakfast/social; Sunday leans recovery/wellness, but both can flex.

Marketing: Sell it to prospective partners as guaranteed engaged attention from a captive, athletic audience — not a sponsorship banner. Promote to members as a rotating perk ("this week's activation: local coffee roaster") to keep the format from feeling repetitive.

Leagues & competitive formats

Ladies Ladder Tournament — new

Weekly, Wed 9:00–10:30am · 3 of 6 courts · women-only standing ladder Target: 12–18 players · Level 1.5–3.2

How it's run: Coordinator-run; players challenge up/down the ladder each week with published standings, so it feels like a running competition rather than a one-off event.

Marketing: Target daytime/flex-schedule women specifically. Market as a standing weekly fixture — "same time every week, same community" — and cross-promote with the Wednesday evening Women's League as a daytime-to-evening pathway.

Box League

8-wk season, Thu 7–8:30pm · 3 of 6 courts · in-house co-ed, level flights Target: 16–24 players · Level 2.2+

How it's run: Coordinator-run; no partner needed, the exact feature that made this the highest-volume league at the tennis clubs it's modeled on.

Marketing: Target Progression/Performance cohort graduates and regular Open Play attendees. Sell "your season, your rank, your box" — movement between boxes each cycle is the reason to renew.

Women's League

8-wk season, Wed 7–8:30pm (prime time) · 3 of 6 courts · round robin Target: 16–20 players · Level 2.0+

How it's run: Coordinator-run, deliberately scheduled in the same prime evening slot as the co-ed leagues rather than off-peak.

Marketing: Target Ladies Ladder participants and clinic graduates directly. The hook is the prime-time placement itself — signals this is a first-class program, not an afterthought.

Corporate League

8-wk season, Tue 12:00–1:15pm · 2 of 6 courts · round robin, 6–10 teams Target: 24–40 players · Open, all levels

How it's run: Coordinator-run; teams of 4 register together and play through the lunch hour, so no evening commitment is required from participants.

Marketing: Target local company HR/wellness budgets with a per-team flat fee ($600). Position as a turnkey team-building product; bundle an Intro clinic for new-to-padel employees.

Ladder (ongoing, app-based)

Always-on, quarterly access · 2 of 6 reserved courts Sat 11am–1pm + informal use all week Target: 20–30 active players · Level 3.0+

How it's run: Self-managed via app/leaderboard; players schedule their own challenge matches with minimal staff involvement beyond the reserved weekend window.

Marketing: Target competitive players who've outgrown drop-in Open Play but aren't ready for a full league season. Promote the "always-on" flexibility as the hook.

King of the Court

Monthly event, Sat 1–2:30pm · 4 of 6 courts · winners up, losers down Target: 16 players/event · Level 2.5+

How it's run: Coordinator-run event with a built-in energy spike — a strong candidate for music, spectators, and social content since it's visually dynamic.

Marketing: Target the whole member base as a monthly can't-miss event. Use it as the most shareable, social-content-friendly program to drive social reach.

Masters / 50+ Clinic & Ladder

90 min, Sun 11am–12:30pm · 2 of 6 courts · lower intensity Target: 8–12 players · All levels, 50+

How it's run: Coach-led for the clinic portion, self-managed ladder challenges layered on top; paced deliberately slower than the daytime clinics.

Marketing: Target the 50+ demographic through community/senior-center partnerships and word of mouth. Message around longevity, low-impact cardio, and a built-in social group rather than competition.

Retiree League — new

8-wk season, Tue/Thu 2:00–3:30pm · 2 of 6 courts · round robin Target: 12–16 players · Level 1.5–3.0

How it's run: Coordinator-run, relaxed pace, sits in the biggest dead zone on the calendar — weekday early afternoon, when courts would otherwise sit empty for hours.

Marketing: Target retirees and anyone with a flexible weekday schedule directly — community centers, 55+ neighborhoods, word of mouth. Message: "your weekday game, same time every week, no evening commitment required."

Industry League — new

8-wk season, Tue/Thu 10:30am–12:00pm · 2 of 6 courts · round robin Target: 12–16 players · Level 1.5–3.0

How it's run: Coordinator-run, scheduled in the late-morning dead zone before lunch — exactly when restaurant, bar, and service-industry staff are free before evening shifts start.

Marketing: Target restaurant/bar/hospitality staff directly through nearby venues, industry Facebook/Instagram groups, and staff word of mouth. Message: "you're off in the morning — come play." Consider a service-industry discount or partner-venue cross-promotion.

Bronze / Silver / Gold League — new, 2 nights/week each

8-wk season · round robin · one league per level band, each running two nights a week instead of one Target: 24–32 players each per season (12–16 per night) · Bronze = Level A, Silver = Level B, Gold = Level C

How it's run: Coordinator-run. Every league night is deliberately scheduled opposite that level's two Developmental Clinic days, never on top of them — Level A's clinics are Mon/Wed, so Bronze runs Fri (3 courts) + Sat (4 courts); Level B's are Wed/Fri, so Silver runs Mon (3 courts) + Thu (3 courts); Level C's are Thu/Sat, so Gold runs Tue (3 courts) + Sun (4 courts). This replaces the generic "Extended Social Play" filler blocks that used to sit in those same evening windows — the court time is the same, but it's now a structured, sellable league product instead of unstructured drop-in play. Bronze's Saturday night and Gold's Sunday night land on days those levels already have Social Play, so it reads as "stay a little later"; Silver's Thursday night is a genuinely new weekly visit for Level B, which is the trade-off for giving every level a real two-night league instead of one.

Marketing: Pitch directly to Developmental Clinic members at their level: "you're already here Monday for Social — Silver League is right after." Players can register for either night, or both if their schedule allows — a second registration option worth surfacing at sign-up. The offset-from-clinic-days framing is also the retention story: it's extra distinct touchpoints in the week without ever colliding with that level's own clinic time.

Developmental Clinics — 4-week rotation, offset by day (Level A/B/C)

Each level meets twice a week — Level A: Monday AM + Wednesday PM; Level B: Wednesday AM + Friday PM; Level C: Thursday AM + Saturday late-AM (see Weekly Calendar) — always at a 4:1 ratio. Both of a level's days teach from the same 4-theme technical bank, but Day 2 always starts two weeks ahead of Day 1 in the cycle. The payoff: attend only Day 1 for 4 weeks and you get all 4 themes; attend only Day 2 for 4 weeks, same thing, just in a different order; attend both in the same week and you never repeat a theme, you just get to the full arc twice as fast. This is the same underlying 4-theme bank as the old rotating clinics, now applied consistently across every level.

Implementation checklist

  • Hire coaches: 3 minimum to open, not 2 — the 4:1 ratio on Developmental Clinics means a full 2-court session needs 2 coaches at once, so a 2-coach team has zero slack the moment one clinic fills up.
  • Assign clinic ownership: map each coach to specific days against this calendar (e.g. Coach 1 — Level A days: Mon AM + Wed PM + Foundations Gen 1 (Mon) & Gen 2 (Wed); Coach 2 — Level B days: Wed AM + Fri PM + Progression Gen 1 (Tue) & Gen 2 (Fri); Coach 3 — Level C days: Thu AM + Sat late-AM + Performance Gen 1 (Thu) & Gen 2 (Tue) + overflow/2nd-coach coverage on full clinics).
  • Recruit each Seasonal cohort's two generations separately: Foundations/Progression/Performance now sell two parallel intakes per level per season — track fill rate per generation, not just per level, and flag the "double-pace" add-on at signup for anyone asking about scheduling conflicts.
  • Cross-train coordinators: assign 1–2 front-desk/ops staff to run Social play, Box League, Women's League, Corporate League, Ladder, King of the Court, the new Ladies Ladder, Retiree, Industry, Bronze, Silver, and Gold Leagues, and the 2 weekend Vendor Activations (≈30 hrs/week combined) — this role does not require a certified coach.
  • Recruit the Retiree League roster: target 12–16 players, level 1.5–3.0, through community/senior-center partnerships — this fills the weekday-afternoon dead zone (Tue/Thu 2:00–3:30pm) that was previously fully open.
  • Recruit the Industry League roster: target 12–16 players, level 1.5–3.0, through nearby restaurant/bar/hospitality venues and staff word of mouth — this fills the weekday-morning dead zone (Tue/Thu 10:30am–12:00pm) before the lunch block.
  • Configure court-blocking in the booking system: reserve the exact court counts per slot shown on the calendar (e.g. 2 of 6 for Foundations Mon 5:30–7pm, 1 of 6 for that day's Trio Pod), releasing the remainder as open booking automatically.
  • Build the Trio Pod signup flow: trios register as a pre-formed group of 3, capture their shared level and pick one of the three weekly windows (Mon 9am / Wed 5:30pm / Sat 8am).
  • Set up Ladies Ladder Tournament registration and scoring: ladder app or the booking platform's league module, published standings, Wednesday 9:00–10:30am.
  • Finalize and print the four Generations curricula (Foundations, Progression, Performance, Trio Pod) as session-by-session lesson plans — see the guide above — and have every coach review their assigned curriculum before their first cohort starts.
  • Load the full 6-court weekly grid into the booking software and confirm the programmed/open court counts match this plan exactly before publishing the calendar publicly.
  • Sequence launch marketing: lead with an on-demand Intro to Padel booking, the Wed/Fri 5:30pm Developmental Clinic → 7:00pm Social pairing, and the new Ladies Ladder Tournament — these are the fastest, lowest-friction entry points.
  • Track utilization from week one: programmed-vs-open court ratio per slot, seasonal cohort fill rate, Trio Pod signups, and each coach's clinic hours vs. private-lesson hours.
  • Revisit court allocation quarterly: shift courts from under-filled programmed slots to open booking (or the reverse) based on real attendance data, not assumptions.
  • Move Intro to Padel to on-demand booking: configure it in the booking system exactly like a private lesson (any coach, any open court, first-timer flow) instead of a published weekly slot.
  • Build the 3 Purpose Clinic curricula: Cardio Padel, Strength Padel, and HIIT Padel — each a fixed weekly slot rather than a rotation — as session plans a coach can run without prep.
  • Build the Developmental Clinic Day-1/Day-2 offset schedule: print the 4-week rotation table (see Seasonal curriculum guide) with Day-1 and Day-2 columns clearly separated, and confirm every coach knows which column applies to their assigned day before the first week goes live.
  • Recruit the Bronze/Silver/Gold League rosters: target 24–32 players each per season (12–16 per night, across each league's two weekly nights) at the matching level band, pitched directly to Developmental Clinic members as "your league is right after your usual Social night" — see the League playbook card above for the exact framing.
  • Secure weekend vendor partners: line up a rotating roster for Saturday's Breakfast & Padel Activation (coffee/breakfast, local restaurants) and Sunday's Recovery & Wellness Activation (recovery brand, nutrition, gear) — aim for a full month booked in advance.
  • Plan the Friday Themed Rotation calendar: assign the 8 themes (Ladies Night, Industry Night, Couples/Date Night, Trivia Night, Retro/Decade Night, Guest Pro Exhibition, Vinyl/DJ Social, Charity/Community Night) to specific Fridays for the next two quarters, and book any guest pro appearances or nonprofit partners early.
  • Test and track the weekend-morning court split: compare attendance/fill-rate for the new Sat/Sun court-shared slots (Purpose Clinic + Ladder Challenge + Trio Pod; Developmental Clinic Level C Day 2 + King of the Court) against the old sequential layout, and adjust the court split after 4–6 weeks of data.
  • Assign a partnerships lead: one person owns outreach to the Fitness/Longevity/F&B/Lifestyle activation targets and the fitness-club and corporate reciprocity lists below — without a single owner, this list becomes a wish list instead of a pipeline.
  • Draft a one-page reciprocal-partnership template: guest-pass counts each direction, one joint social post, one co-hosted event per quarter — so every outreach conversation starts from the same simple ask instead of being negotiated from scratch each time.

Activations, vendor & community partnerships

The calendar already has two standing Activation slots (Sat 12:00–1:30pm Breakfast & Padel, Sun 12:30–2:00pm Recovery & Wellness — see Weekly Calendar and Weekend Mornings & Vendor Activations above). This section is the deeper idea bank and Denver-specific target list behind those slots, organized into four categories so the rotation stays varied instead of becoming "the same coffee cart every month." Each category lists several distinct activation formats plus real local partner candidates already running businesses built for exactly this kind of pop-up/guest-visit collaboration.

Fitness & Movement

Corporate partnerships

Two different tracks live here: selling Arenna's courts and coaching to companies as events and standing programs, and setting up reciprocal member-perk partnerships with other fitness/racquet businesses and with corporates across industries — so Arenna members get real discounts elsewhere, and those companies' employees get a reason to try Arenna.

Corporate event & program offerings — selling to companies

Corporate Team-Building Tournament

Half-day event · round robin + intro clinic for non-players · catered

How it's run: A coordinator plus 1–2 coaches run a simplified bracket so total beginners and experienced employees can play side by side; a short intro clinic warms up anyone who's never held a paddle before the tournament starts.

Pitch: An active, genuinely fun alternative to the usual escape-room or ropes-course team-building day — works for mixed-fitness groups because the format scales from total beginner to competitive in the same event.

Dedicated Corporate League (expanded)

Builds on the existing Tuesday 12:00–1:15pm Corporate League

How it's run: Add a 2nd flight or evening option once a company has 10+ employees signed up, with optional company-branded jerseys or court signage for the season.

Pitch: Position as an easy add-on for companies already sending employees to the lunchtime league — the natural next step is "let's get your own team/flight."

Company-Branded Generations Cohort

6–8 weeks · same Foundations/Progression/Performance curriculum, private group

How it's run: A company buys out a full seasonal cohort exclusively for its employees, scheduled around the company's preferred day/time, taught from the exact same curriculum as the public Generations program (see Seasonal curriculum guide).

Pitch: Frame it as an employee wellness benefit line item — easier for an HR department to approve as "benefit spend" than an ad-hoc one-off event.

Executive Client Entertainment Package

90 min private court block + coach-led mini clinic + catered lounge

How it's run: A private, guided session designed for hosting clients rather than internal team building — low-pressure enough for non-athletic guests, active and memorable enough to stand out.

Pitch: An alternative to the steakhouse dinner or golf outing for client relationship-building — especially strong for professional-services and finance clients (see the corporate list below).

Corporate Wellness Day Add-on

60–90 min session bundled into an existing company offsite

How it's run: A single flat-rate group session slotted into a company's existing wellness day or offsite agenda — minimal setup required from the client's HR/events team.

Pitch: The lowest-friction entry point on this list — a single add-on line item rather than a new standalone event to sell and plan from scratch.
